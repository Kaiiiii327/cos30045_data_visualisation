Australian Television Energy Data

Choosing an Energy-Smart TV in Australia

This data story uses Australian Government television energy-rating data processed in KNIME to help consumers understand the television choices available in Australia and how screen size and screen technology relate to energy consumption.

AUDIENCE

Who is this story for?

This story is designed for Australian consumers who are planning to buy a television.

These consumers may want to understand which screen technologies and sizes are commonly available, which brands provide more model choices, and how these characteristics relate to energy use.

The information is presented using simple visual comparisons so that consumers can understand the main findings without needing detailed technical knowledge.

01

What Screen Technologies Are Available?

What type of TV screen technologies are available in Australia and which are the most frequent?

The dataset contains three main screen technologies: LCD, LCD (LED), and OLED.

LCD (LED) televisions dominate the dataset with 3,485 models (84.53%). LCD accounts for 356 models (8.63%), while OLED represents 282 models (6.84%).

This means consumers searching for a television in Australia are much more likely to encounter LCD (LED) models than the other screen technologies represented in the dataset.

02

What Screen Sizes Are Most Common?

What screen sizes are available and which sizes appear most frequently?

Television sizes vary significantly across the dataset. However, some standard screen-size categories appear much more frequently than others.

The KNIME result shows that the 55-inch category is the most common, while several mid-range screen sizes around the 40–50 inch range are also strongly represented.

This suggests that consumers have many screen-size options, but medium-to-large televisions make up a substantial part of the available market.

03

Which Brands Offer the Most Model Choices?

Which television brands have the largest number of models in the dataset?

The number of models differs considerably between television brands.

Samsung Electronics has the largest number of models with 859, followed by Kogan with 702 and LG with 682.

These brands therefore provide a wider range of model choices within this dataset compared with many other brands.

Availability is only part of the decision

Screen technology, screen size and brand show consumers what choices are available. However, another important consideration is how these television characteristics relate to energy consumption.

04

Which Screen Technology Uses the Least Typical Power?

How does median average-mode power differ between screen technologies?

The KNIME analysis compares the median average-mode power of LCD, LCD (LED), and OLED televisions.

LCD televisions have the lowest median value at approximately 69.62.

LCD (LED) televisions have a median of approximately 107.58, while OLED televisions have a median of approximately 113.70.

In this dataset, LCD therefore has the lowest typical average-mode power consumption among the three screen technologies.

05

Does a Bigger TV Use More Power?

What is the relationship between television screen size and average-mode power?

The scatter plot shows a clear positive relationship between screen size and power consumption.

As television screen size increases, average-mode power generally increases as well.

This is an important consideration for consumers who are deciding between smaller and larger televisions, because a larger screen will generally require more power according to this dataset.

06

Does Screen Size Determine Star Rating?

What is the relationship between television screen size and energy star rating?

The star ratings are distributed across many different screen sizes.

Unlike the relationship between screen size and power consumption, the chart does not show a strong simple relationship between screen size and star rating.

Consumers should therefore check the energy rating of individual television models rather than assuming that a particular screen size automatically has a better or worse star rating.

What Should Consumers Take Away?

The Australian television dataset shows that LCD (LED) televisions dominate the available models, while the 55-inch category is one of the most common screen sizes.

However, popularity does not automatically mean lower energy consumption. The analysis shows that larger televisions generally consume more power, and typical power consumption also differs between screen technologies.

When choosing a television, consumers should therefore compare screen size, screen technology and the energy rating of individual models rather than making a decision based only on popularity or size.
