Television Dataset

Television Energy Insights

The following visualisations were created from Australian television energy rating data processed using KNIME.

01

Available Screen Technologies

What type of TV screen technologies are currently available in Australia and which are the most frequent?

The dataset contains LCD, LCD (LED) and OLED television technologies. From the visualisation, LCD (LED) appears to be the most frequently represented screen technology in the dataset.

02

Screen Size Distribution

What screen sizes are available and which are most frequent?

Television screen sizes vary considerably across the dataset. The distribution shows that some common screen size categories occur much more frequently than others.

03

Brands with the Largest Number of Models

Which brands have the largest number of television models?

The chart compares the number of models registered under different television brands. Some brands have a much larger number of models represented in the dataset.

04

Screen Technology and Power Consumption

Which type of screen technology consumes the least amount of power?

The chart compares the median average-mode power consumption for LCD, LCD (LED) and OLED televisions. The visualisation can be used to identify which screen technology has the lowest typical power consumption.

05

Screen Size vs Power Consumption

What is the relationship between screen size and power use?

The scatter plot shows a clear positive relationship. Larger television screens generally use more average-mode power than smaller television screens.

06

Screen Size vs Star Rating

What is the relationship between star rating and screen size?

The scatter plot compares energy star ratings across different screen sizes. Star ratings vary within similar screen sizes, suggesting that screen size alone does not determine the energy efficiency rating.

07

Power Consumption Between Brands

Are there differences in power consumption between brands?

The box plot shows variation in average-mode power consumption between television brands. The distributions differ between brands, and several high-power outliers can also be observed.

Key Observation

The visualisations show that television characteristics such as screen size, screen technology and brand are associated with differences in energy consumption. In particular, the data suggests that larger televisions generally require more power.
